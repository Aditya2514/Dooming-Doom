USP. Reinforcement learning from scratch spends most of a laptop budget discovering that walking forward is useful. We instead distil a privileged teacher into a sensor-only student with DAgger. The teacher may see the floor plan and its true pose, but its decisions are deliberately imitable: it explores toward places its rays have not swept, targets keys once seen and the exit once it has been on camera, fights and dodges what is visible, and presses USE once per door. The student is a recurrent network with a dead-reckoned spatial memory and a small RGB exit detector; it learns, while driving itself, to recover from its own mistakes. Every action at evaluation comes from the network: no rules, no search, no aiming code in submission/.
Left: the acting network (all of it ships; the auxiliary exit head and the value head are training-only). Right: how it is trained.

1 · Sensory representation

  • Rays: 64 × 360°, distance + one-hot hit type; Conv1d with circular padding (ray 63 neighbours ray 0).
  • Entities: ≤32 visible objects (type, distance, bearing, relative velocity); masked self-attention + attention pooling with a learned null token: any number of enemies, order-independent.
  • Scalars: vitals, key flags, own velocity, last buttons (USE fires on the button-down edge), elapsed time.
  • RGB: the 160×120 frame, 4×4-pooled to 30×40, through a 56k-parameter CNN. Rays and entities have no exit class, but exits are visible: the official sample map's exit is a red GATE3 floor pad.

One shared adapter feeds training and DoomAgent; tests check step-by-step acting equals whole-episode training (1e-5).

2 · Architecture and actions

Five encoders → fusion → GRU 256 → a categorical over 16 macro-actions (forward(+turn), turn, fire(+turn), forward+USE, strafe, back(+fire), …). Nine independent buttons allow contradictions (forward+back) and USE spam that bounces doors; macros remove these and give imitation one clean label per step. Inference runs on CPU (≈4.7 ms), avoiding GPU contention.

3 · Spatial memory design

  • Dead reckoning: heading from its own TURN buttons (engine turn rates, ≤3.5° error); position from the velocity in vitals (least-squares fit): median drift ≈55 units after 100 decisions, ≈100 after 300 (1–2 map cells).
  • SpatialMap: 320×320 cells of 64 units in the episode's own frame, written every decision: visited, swept by rays, door seen, locked door seen.
  • Read: a 24×24 heading-up crop through a small CNN, fused before the GRU; "unexplored space to my left" and "the locked door I passed" look the same on every map. It is preprocessing + learned memory; nothing searches it.

4 · Training and exploration pipeline

  • Teacher (training/expert.py), priority per decision: dodge (a visible projectile will pass within 56 units → strafe) → fight (nearest visible enemy) → door (one USE, then wait: USE on a moving door reverses it) → unstick → go to: a seen key > the exit once on camera > the nearest cell its rays have not swept. Locked doors are walls until their key is held, so "find key → return → exit" emerges from exploration. Its state follows the executed actions, so its labels stay correct when the student drives.
  • DAgger: the teacher drives first; then student-only and mixed episodes (teacher share β annealed), every visited state labelled; cross-entropy on whole episodes through the GRU (chunked backprop, fp16), FIFO dataset up to 1M states.
  • RGB exit detector: the RGB branch is first trained alone (training/pretrain_rgb.py, 90k teacher frames) to say whether the exit is on screen and where; it then joins with zero fusion weights (the policy is unchanged at first) and DAgger continues with that auxiliary loss.
  • Maps: our deterministic UDMF generator: combat arenas (with an exit: killing every enemy does not end a map), multi-room, key-door (A key on the way, B locked door first, C dead-end key), mixed gauntlets; half the exits are red floor pads; random wall/floor textures. 2,140 training maps; validation, test and confirmation pools use disjoint seeds and layouts.

7 · What we measured and changed

  • Teacher labels must follow executed actions. A first DAgger run collapsed to 0% exits: a student turning in place was labelled "back up", learned it, and looped. Tracking what was really executed fixed it (regression test).
  • Floor-pad exits. The student killed the official map's monster and opened its door in 10/10 runs but found the floor pad in 1/10; red pads, an exit-on-camera teacher rule and the RGB branch took pad exits from 0.48 to 0.78.
  • Pistol tap-fire (held fire spreads): teacher survival 91% tapping vs 99% holding fire, so it holds fire.

8 · Reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (implemented, measured)

training/finetune.py: recurrent PPO from the imitation policy (GAE, clipped objective, stored GRU states with in-sequence resets, critic warm-up, KL anchor to the imitation policy) with scorer-aligned rewards and a count-based curiosity bonus (0.04/√n on entering a cell of the agent's own spatial map). In three configurations it lowered held-out exits (e.g. 0.58 → 0.38), most likely because ~2.5k decisions per update give noisy advantages that erode a finely imitated policy. We ship the DAgger policy; reward-weighted imitation on the saved DAgger dataset is the next step.

9 · Engineering

10 engines in lockstep with the student batched on GPU; mixed precision cut an update from 3.06 s to 0.24 s; chunked backprop bounds GPU memory (1.1 GB); bit-packed maps (288 bytes per step); a Windows engine-latency fix (3× faster); a live viewer of student vs teacher on held-out maps (training/watch.py).

Rules. submission/ contains only the adapter, the network and the action table; a test checks it never imports training/. The teacher, its BFS planner, the auxiliary exit labels and all ground-truth rewards exist only at training time; the RGB frame is part of the official observation.
