Dooming Doom: a sensor-only DOOM agent distilled from a privileged teacher

Yugaantar 2026 · DevForge · Track 1 "Can It Play DOOM?" · repository Aditya2514/Dooming-Doom · numbers are on held-out maps never trained on
USP. Learning DOOM from reward alone spends most of a laptop budget discovering that walking forward is useful; our first agent (PPO from scratch, 9 independent buttons) ended up firing 42% of the time whether or not an enemy was ahead. We instead distil a privileged teacher into a sensor-only student with DAgger. The teacher may see the floor plan and its true position, but its decisions are deliberately imitable: it explores toward places its rays have not swept, targets keys and the exit only once seen, fights and dodges what is visible, and presses USE once per door. The student is a recurrent network with a dead-reckoned spatial memory, sees only the official observation, and learns, while driving itself, to recover from its own mistakes. Every action at evaluation comes from the network: no rules, no search, no aiming code in submission/.
Left: the acting network (all of it ships). Right: how it is trained. Orange boxes are what runs at evaluation.

1 · Sensory representation

  • Rays: 64 × 360°, distance + one-hot hit type; Conv1d with circular padding (ray 63 neighbours ray 0).
  • Entities: ≤32 visible objects (type, distance, bearing, relative velocity); masked self-attention + attention pooling with a learned null token: any number of enemies, order-independent.
  • Scalars: vitals, key flags, own velocity, last buttons (USE fires on the button-down edge), elapsed time. No pixels: rays and entities already summarise geometry.

One shared adapter feeds training and DoomAgent; tests check step-by-step acting equals whole-episode training (1e-5).

2 · Neural architecture and actions

Encoders → fusion → GRU 256 → a categorical over 16 macro-actions (forward(+turn), turn, fire(+turn), forward+USE, strafe, back(+fire), …). Nine independent buttons allow contradictions (forward+back) and USE spam that bounces doors; macros remove these and give imitation one clean label per step. Inference on CPU (≈3–5 ms) avoids GPU contention.

3 · Spatial memory design

There are no coordinates in the observation, so the agent builds its own map:

  • Dead reckoning: heading from its own TURN buttons (engine turn rates, ≤3.5° error); position from the velocity in vitals (least-squares fit on validation episodes): median drift ≈55 units after 100 decisions, ≈100 after 300 (1–2 map cells).
  • SpatialMap: 320×320 cells of 64 units in the episode's own frame, 4 channels written every decision: visited, swept by rays (≤768 units), door seen, locked door seen.
  • Read: a 24×24 heading-up crop (1,536 units across) through a small CNN, fused before the GRU. "Unexplored space to my left" and "the locked door I passed" look the same on every map; the GRU carries the rest across the whole level.
  • It is preprocessing + learned memory (explicitly allowed); the map never chooses an action and nothing searches it.

4 · Training and exploration pipeline

  • Teacher (training/expert.py), priority per decision: dodge (a visible projectile will pass within 56 units in 40 tics → strafe off its line) → fight (nearest visible enemy: turn, fire when on target) → door (one USE, then wait: USE on a moving door reverses it) → unstick → go to: a seen key still needed > the exit once seen > a useful visible pickup > the nearest cell its rays have not swept (frontier exploration). Locked doors are walls until their key is held, so "find key → return to the locked door → exit" emerges from exploration. Teacher on held-out maps: 100% exits on rooms, key-door and mixed maps; combat 97% survival.
  • DAgger: iteration 0 the teacher drives (200k labelled states); afterwards 40% of episodes are student-only (on-policy states and an honest running score) and the rest mix teacher/student per step (β 0.5→0.1). Every visited state is labelled. Cross-entropy on whole episodes through the GRU (chunked backprop, fp16), FIFO dataset of 1M states.
  • Exploration is learned from the teacher's frontier behaviour on the ray-swept map, the same quantity the student's spatial map holds; the student never needs a hand-written exploration rule.
  • Maps: our deterministic UDMF generator — combat arenas, multi-room, key-door (A key on the way, B locked door first, C dead-end key), easy key-door, mixed gauntlets (enemies + 1–2 locks) — and floor-pad exits like the official sample map. Train / validation / test / pad-exit pools use disjoint seeds and reject shared layouts; 2,140 training maps in total.

6 · What we measured and changed

  • Teacher labels must follow executed actions. Our first DAgger run collapsed to 0% exits at iteration 2: the teacher tracked its own suggestions, so a student turning in place looked "stuck" and was labelled "back up", which the student learned, which looked stuck again. Tracking what was really executed fixed it (regression test); stalls fell from 33% to 0%.
  • Floor-pad exits. The official sample map exits through a 64×64 floor pad, invisible to rays; all our exits were alcoves. The student opened the door and killed the monster in 10/10 runs but found the pad in 1/10. We added pad exits to the generator and a held-out pad pool to measure it.
  • Pistol tap-fire (an engine quirk: held fire spreads). On 80 matched episodes teacher survival was 91% tapping always, 93% tapping beyond 512 units, 99% holding fire; rate of fire wins at DOOM's distances, so the teacher holds fire.
  • All enemies dead does not end a map in the real engine: training episodes continue so the agent keeps exploring.

7 · Reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (implemented, measured)

training/finetune.py: recurrent PPO from the imitation policy (GAE, clipped objective, stored GRU states with in-sequence resets, value-head warm-up, a decaying teacher cross-entropy against forgetting, KL early stop) with scorer-aligned rewards and a count-based curiosity bonus (0.04/√n on entering a cell of the agent's own spatial map). Within our budget it lowered held-out exits (0.58 → 0.38 after ~34 policy updates, in two runs with different settings), most likely because advantages from a freshly trained critic are noise that erodes a finely imitated policy faster than reward improves it. We therefore ship the DAgger policy; a better-initialised critic and far more steps are the next step.

8 · Engineering

10 engines in lockstep with the student batched on GPU; mixed precision cut an update from 3.06 s to 0.24 s; whole-episode batches overflowed the 4 GB GPU into system RAM (14 GB), fixed with chunked backprop (peak 1.1 GB); bit-packed maps (288 bytes per step); a Windows engine-latency fix (3× faster).
